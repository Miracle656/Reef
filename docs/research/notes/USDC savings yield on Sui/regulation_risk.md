# Regulatory & Risk Landscape: Consumer USDC Yield/Savings (Nigeria-first + global), as of 2026-11-02

> Research notes, not legal advice. Items under "Inferences" or tagged [background, unverified this session] come from prior knowledge and were not re-confirmed by a source fetched here. Sources disagree on several Nigerian figures (noted inline).

## Q1. Nigeria: SEC / ISA 2025, VASP licensing, ARIP, and whether USDC yield is a CIS or deposit-taking

### Takeaway
Under the Investments and Securities Act 2025, virtual/digital assets are "securities", so the SEC regulates stablecoins and VASPs. SEC's **proposed** Rules on Digital and Virtual Asset Operations, Custody and Markets (published 20 Aug 2026, not yet adopted as of the latest sources found) explicitly bring **staking, lending, yield products, liquidity pools and non-custodial wallet services** into scope. They also apply to anyone serving Nigerian residents "through digital channels" and route new entrants through ARIP. A retail USDC "earn" product aimed at Lagos users is therefore squarely inside the SEC perimeter, whatever its custody model. Pooled, managed yield also looks like a collective investment scheme (CIS).

### Cited Findings
**ISA 2025 basics**
- ISA 2025 commenced 25 Mar 2025 and was gazetted 2 May 2025. Section 357 defines "securities" to include virtual and digital assets alongside investment contracts — [CryptoSlate law profile](https://cryptoslate.com/crypto-laws/nigeria-investments-securities-act-2025-virtual-digital-assets/); [PwC Nigeria summary](https://www.pwc.com/ng/en/publications/summary-of-key-changes-investments-securities-act-2025.html). (Section numbers come from secondary sources. Check them against the gazetted text.)
- s.3(3)(i) empowers the SEC to regulate VASPs, digital asset operators and exchanges. Because virtual assets are securities, stablecoins "fall squarely within" SEC jurisdiction. ISA also requires "exchanges" to keep an Investor Protection Fund, though how that applies to digital asset exchanges is unclear. The fund covers insolvency, negligence or misappropriation, not market or performance losses — [Aluko & Oyebode, "Do stablecoins qualify as securities", 17 Jun 2025](https://aluko-oyebode.com/insights/do-stable-coins-qualify-as-securities-under-nigerian-law/)
- The 2025 exposure-draft VASP rules (cl. 29.1) let only financial institutions issue stablecoins. Issuers need a CBN no-objection plus SEC registration, and reserves must be held in a trust account at a custodian bank — [Aluko & Oyebode](https://aluko-oyebode.com/insights/do-stable-coins-qualify-as-securities-under-nigerian-law/)
- **CIS:** s.150 defines a collective investment scheme broadly ("in whatever form", open- or close-ended). The Act expressly prohibits Ponzi schemes and other unregistered investment schemes and penalises their promoters. Foreign schemes targeting Nigerian investors must register with the SEC — [Mondaq: ISA 2025 for private capital](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/venture-capital/1638148/nigerias-investments-and-securities-act-2025-what-is-changing-for-private-capital-players); [Chambers: SEC role mitigating Ponzi schemes](https://chambers.com/articles/strengthening-investor-protection-in-nigeria); [Afriwise: ISA 2025 clampdown on illegal operators](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/beyond-the-headlines-the-isa-2025-and-the-sec-clampdown-on-illegal-market-operators)
- After CBEX, reporting said unregistered digital investment platforms face up to a 10-year jail term under ISA 2025 — [Leadership.ng](https://leadership.ng/unregistered-digital-investment-platforms-face-10-yr-jail-term-sec-cracks-down-amid-outrage-over-cbex-crash) (penalty figure is from press, not verified against the Act).

**SEC capital circular (Jan 2026)**
- SEC Circular 26-1 (Jan 2026) raised minimum capital. Digital asset exchanges and custodians go from ₦500m to ₦2bn, and offering platforms to ₦1bn. One table lists Ancillary VASP at ₦300m, Digital Asset Intermediary and Digital Asset Platform Operator at ₦500m each, and RWA tokenisation at ₦1bn. The compliance deadline is 30 Jun 2027 — [Nairametrics, 16 Jan 2026](https://nairametrics.com/2026/01/16/sec-raises-capital-requirement-for-crypto-exchanges-in-nigeria-to-n2-billion/); [Mondaq/Tunde & Adisa](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/fin-tech/1734232/fintech-regulatory-update-sec-minimum-capital-requirements-for-virtual-assets-service-providers-and-fintech-operators); [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/bd-weekender/article/higher-capital-thresholds-and-new-vasp-licences-key-takeaways-from-the-nigerian-securities-and-exchange-commissions-january-2026-circular/)

**Proposed Rules on Digital and Virtual Asset Operations, Custody and Markets 2026 (draft)**
- The SEC published the proposal for consultation. If adopted, it supersedes the 2022 Digital Asset Rules and all prior digital-asset circulars. It is an 18-part framework — [SEC Nigeria proposal page](https://sec.gov.ng/for-investors/proposed-rules-digital-and-virtual-assets-operations-custody-and-markets/); [PDF](https://sec.gov.ng/documents/1503/Proposed_Rules_on_Digital_and_Virtual_Asset_Operations_Custody_and_Markets.pdf); [Aluko & Oyebode alert, 11 Sep 2026](https://aluko-oyebode.com/insights/nigerias-digital-asset-regulatory-reset/)
- Commentary says the draft explicitly covers **staking, lending, yield products, liquidity pools, P2P/OTC and non-custodial wallet services**, which were previously grey areas — [The Condia](https://thecondia.com/sec-rules-could-shrink-nigeria-crypto-market/); [Afriwise: regulatory reset](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/nigerias-digital-asset-regulatory-reset). The specific conditions for yield products were **not** extracted in this session and should be read from the SEC PDF.
- The draft applies to anyone serving Nigerian residents "through digital channels". It states that no one may conduct digital asset business in Nigeria "or targeted at persons resident in Nigeria" without SEC registration. Applicants must be Nigerian-incorporated, with a Nigerian registered office and resident CEO and principal officers. Foreign issuers may need a local entity — [Afriwise: proposed rules](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/nigerias-proposed-rules-on-digital-and-virtual-assets-key-provisions-and-implication-for-businesses); [KuCoin news summary](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/nigeria-s-sec-proposes-local-office-and-capital-requirements-for-crypto-firms)
- **ARIP:** applicants first enter the Accelerated Regulatory Incubation Programme and may get an Approval-in-Principle valid for two years before full registration. Registered CMOs, registered VASPs and subsidiaries of licensed financial institutions may skip ARIP — [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/nigerias-proposed-rules-on-digital-and-virtual-assets-key-provisions-and-implication-for-businesses)
- The draft sets a ₦30m registration fee and tiered capital: exchanges and custodians ₦2bn, platform operators, offering and tokenisation platforms ₦500m, general VASP ₦200m. These figures conflict with Circular 26-1 — [Leadership.ng](https://leadership.ng/sec-proposes-n30m-registration-fee-n2bn-capital-requirement-for-crypto-exchanges/); [Arise TV](https://www.arise.tv/sec-proposes-n2bn-capital-n30m-fee-for-crypto-firms/)
- No promotion is allowed for an unregistered asset, and promotions must be "accurate, fair and not misleading". Unbacked stablecoins are prohibited from issuance. Trading entities need a complaints framework — [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/nigerias-proposed-rules-on-digital-and-virtual-assets-key-provisions-and-implication-for-businesses)
- Ancillary VASPs (pure tech-infrastructure providers) were dropped from the proposal. That removes a lighter-touch "we just provide software" category — [Condia / search summary](https://thecondia.com/sec-rules-could-shrink-nigeria-crypto-market/)

### Inferences
- A Nigerian-facing USDC yield feature in ReeF likely needs SEC registration (via ARIP), or a partnership with an SEC-registered VASP or custodian. Being non-custodial does not take it out of scope, because the draft names non-custodial wallet services and yield products, and the "targeted at Nigerian residents" test catches offshore entities.
- A **pooled app vault** (ReeF takes USDC, deploys it, and pays a return) maps closely onto the ISA s.150 CIS definition and the "unregistered investment scheme" offences. That is the highest-risk design.
- **Deposit-taking (BOFIA 2020):** no source found here addressed whether paying yield on stablecoins counts as "banking business". [background, unverified this session] BOFIA reserves banking business, including taking deposits repayable on demand or at fixed term, to CBN-licensed entities. "Withdraw anytime" or "fixed term" plus "savings" or "interest" wording echoes deposit-taking and should be avoided.
- The draft is still unadopted, so its final yield and non-custodial provisions may change. Track the SEC Nigeria site.

### Gaps
- The exact draft-rule text on yield, lending and non-custodial wallets (conditions, disclosures, whether a license category exists for yield routing), the comment deadline, and the adoption date. Read the SEC PDF.
- The ISA 2025 penalty section numbers and amounts for unregistered schemes.
- Any SEC or CBN statement directly on stablecoin yield, and the BOFIA interaction.
- Current ARIP cohort status and which firms (e.g., Quidax, Busha) hold provisional approval. Not re-verified here.

## Q2. Nigeria: CBN stance, cNGN, enforcement precedents (Binance, CBEX), tax

### Takeaway
The CBN reopened bank accounts to VASPs in Dec 2023 under tight conditions, and banks may not trade crypto themselves. cNGN is a bank-consortium naira stablecoin, not a CBDC. Enforcement is aggressive and personal (the Binance executive detention in 2024; CBEX in 2025, followed by account freezes and prosecution threats). The SEC publicly blames unregistered "high-return" platforms. From 1 Jan 2026, crypto gains are taxable income and VASPs have tax registration and reporting duties with heavy penalties.

### Cited Findings
- On 22 Dec 2023 the CBN issued "Guidelines on Operations of Bank Accounts for VASPs", superseding the Feb 2021 restriction. Banks still cannot hold, trade or transact virtual currencies on their own account. Rules include strict KYC/AML, no clearing of third-party cheques, and limits on cash withdrawals (reported as two per quarter) — [Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/money/1571565-cbn-releases-rules-crypto-bank-accounts-ban-cash-withdrawals-others); [Prime Business Africa](https://www.primebusiness.africa/cbn-lifts-restrictions-on-cryptocurrency-transactions-in-banks)
- cNGN is issued by the Africa Stablecoin Consortium (banks and fintechs), piloted in the CBN regulatory sandbox, with launch targeted for 27 Feb 2024. It is not a CBDC — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/nigeria-cbn-greenlights-stablecoin-launch-february); [Nairametrics, 22 Dec 2023](https://nairametrics.com/2023/12/22/nigerian-banks-authorized-to-create-and-manage-stablecoins-cngn/)
- **Binance:** Nigeria detained Binance compliance executive Tigran Gambaryan for about eight months in 2024. The EFCC withdrew money-laundering charges against him in Oct 2024 on health grounds, without acquittal. Charges against Binance itself (money laundering and currency speculation, up to $34.4m) continued — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/322614/nigeria-drops-money-laundering-charges-against-binance-executive-tigran-gambaryan-report); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/nigeria-drops-money-laundering-charges-binance-exec); [DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/people-culture/binance-exec-tigran-gambaryans-nigerian-nightmare-ends)
- **CBEX (Crypto Bridge Exchange):** collapsed around 14–15 Apr 2025 after promising roughly 100% returns. Alleged losses are ₦1.3tn, but another outlet estimated about $12m, so the figures conflict. SEC DG Emomotimi Agama said CBEX "is not registered with us" and "you cannot operate without a license from the SEC". The SEC moved to shut it down with the police and EFCC, then sought freezes of bank accounts and assets tied to ST Technologies International Ltd. Agama also said "we can't regulate what we don't know" — [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/04/15/cbex-ponzi-collapse-nigeria/); [TheCable](https://www.thecable.ng/just-in-cbex-not-registered-with-us-says-sec-dg); [Prime Business Africa](https://www.primebusiness.africa/cbex-platform-operators-will-be-prosecuted-sec); [TechEconomy](https://www.techeconomy.ng/cbex-sec-orders-freeze-of-accounts-over-n1-3-trillion-ponzi-scheme-probe); [Arise TV](https://www.arise.tv/we-cant-regulate-what-we-dont-know-sec-dg-agama-responds-to-cbex-collapse)
- Red flags the public ignored with CBEX included guaranteed high returns, referral rewards and an unregistered operator — [Dubawa](https://dubawa.org/four-red-flags-nigerians-ignored-until-cbex-crashed/). The SEC later warned of CBEX's alleged return — [Ripples Nigeria](https://www.ripplesnigeria.com/sec-warns-nigerians-over-alleged-return-of-cbex/)
- **Tax (Nigeria Tax Act / Nigeria Tax Administration Act 2025, effective 1 Jan 2026):** gains from digital or virtual assets become chargeable gains taxed as income, replacing the 2022 flat 10% CGT. Reported rates conflict (30% for companies; individuals on a progressive scale reported as up to 24% or 25%; one outlet says 15%). One source says crypto losses can't be offset (unverified). Anyone engaged in exchange, trading, custody or issuance must register with the tax authority as a VASP and report customer transaction data. Reported penalty is ₦10m in the first month of default. The regime coincides with OECD CARF from 1 Jan 2026 — [TechCabal, 18 Sep 2025](https://techcabal.com/2025/09/18/nigeria-crypto-tax-law-vasp-penalties-2026/); [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/news/article/tax-overhaul-turns-crypto-from-escape-hatch-to-tax-base/); [Mariblock](https://www.mariblock.com/nigeria-set-to-tax-individual-crypto-transactions-and-exchanges-in-2026); [The Condia: NRS guidelines](https://thecondia.com/nigeria-crypto-virtual-assets-tax-guidelines/)

### Inferences
- In Nigeria, the line between a legitimate yield product and a "CBEX-style" scheme turns on **registration, no fixed or guaranteed returns, and no referral-driven growth**. Referral bonuses tied to deposits are a major red flag here.
- Interest or yield paid to users is likely taxable income for them. If ReeF is a VASP for tax purposes, it inherits reporting duties (NRS/CARF), so its data model must support per-user yield reporting.
- Personal liability for executives is real in Nigeria, as the Gambaryan detention showed. Local principal officers, which the draft rules require, carry that exposure.

### Gaps
- Any 2024–2026 CBN circular specifically on stablecoins or yield (none found). The current cNGN live status and its Sui availability.
- The 2025–2026 outcome of Binance's corporate trial in Nigeria.
- The authoritative crypto tax rate and loss rules from the NRS guideline text.

## Q3. US/global: GENIUS Act, SEC yield precedents, MiCA, geo-blocking

### Takeaway
GENIUS (Public Law 119-27, July 2025) bars **issuers** from paying holders interest or yield. Whether exchanges and third parties can pay "rewards" was still being fought in the CLARITY Act and OCC rulemaking as of Sept 2026, with no confirmed final outcome. US precedent (BlockFi, Coinbase Lend) treats pooled, platform-run lending yield as an unregistered security or investment company. MiCA bans interest on e-money tokens by issuers **and CASPs**, and the EBA and ECB are pushing to extend that to lending and DeFi brokering. For a Nigeria-first app, the practical step is to geo-block the US and EU/EEA from the yield feature.

### Cited Findings
- GENIUS (P.L. 119-27) bars permitted payment stablecoin issuers from paying interest or yield to holders. Exchanges and platforms are not issuers, so commentators read the ban as not reaching them directly. Coinbase pays "rewards" funded partly by Circle sharing reserve interest — [CRS: The Stablecoin Yield Debate](https://www.legistorm.com/reports/view/crs/608234/The_Stablecoin_Yield_Debate.html); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/333472/coinbase-paypal-stablecoin-rewards-genius-prohibitions-how?amp=1); [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/regulation/genius-act-targets-stablecoin-yield-but-workarounds-could-keep-returns-alive)
- The OCC's proposed GENIUS rule (§15.10(c)(4)) creates a rebuttable presumption of violation when an issuer pays an affiliate that passes yield to holders. Community banks want the ban extended to affiliates and partners — [Stablecoin Insider](https://stablecoininsider.org/can-you-still-earn-yield-on-stablecoins-after-the-occs-genius-act-rule/); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/why-us-community-banks-say-the-genius-act-has-a-stablecoin-loophole)
- CLARITY Act status: Senate Banking advanced it 15–9 (May 2026). The Tillis–Alsobrooks compromise would ban rewards "economically or functionally equivalent" to deposit interest but allow activity-based rewards. Cloture was filed 8 Aug 2026 for a 15 Sep procedural vote. ABA and about 80 banking groups lobbied for tighter limits. Galaxy cut its 2026 enactment odds to 30% — [ThinkAdvisor](https://www.thinkadvisor.com/amp/2026/05/14/long-stalled-crypto-bill-wins-key-senate-committee-vote/); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/366680/banks-stay-tight-lipped-as-senators-reveal-proposed-clarity-act-stablecoin-deal); [Stablecoin Insider: cloture](https://stablecoininsider.org/clarity-act-cloture-september-vote/); [Invezz, 14 Sep 2026](https://invezz.com/au/news/2026/09/14/clarity-act-senate-vote-on-tuesday-what-to-expect/); [CoinMarketCap/Galaxy](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/clarity-act-odds-near-zero-after-april-galaxy-warns). **The 15 Sep vote outcome and any enactment are unverified.** One page carried an unsupported "signed into law" banner, which should not be relied on.
- **BlockFi (14 Feb 2022):** paid $50m to the SEC plus $50m to 32 states. The SEC found BlockFi Interest Accounts were securities and that BlockFi was an unregistered investment company for more than 18 months (over 40% of assets in investment securities, i.e., loans to institutional borrowers). It also found false statements about the risk of its loan portfolio. BlockFi stopped offering BIAs to new US investors — [SEC press release 2022-26](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2022-26)
- **Coinbase Lend (Sept 2021):** Coinbase dropped its planned USDC Lend product after the SEC threatened to sue, on the view that it was a security — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/92789/blockfi-pay-100m-penalty-stop-opening-new-high-yield-bitcoin-accounts-report); [Merkle Science](https://www.merklescience.com/blog/blockfi-reaches-a-100-million-settlement-with-the-sec-and-ceases-sales-of-its-lending-product). (Whether this was a formal Wells notice is described inconsistently.)
- **MiCA Art. 50:** EMT issuers **and CASPs** may not grant interest on e-money tokens. "Interest" includes any remuneration or benefit related to how long a holder keeps the token, including via third parties — [ESMA Interactive Single Rulebook, Art. 50](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-50-prohibition-granting-interest). MiCA does not regulate crypto lending as such. The EBA's Sept 2026 response to the MiCA review flags regulatory arbitrage and floats rules for CASPs that broker lending or **connect clients to DeFi lending protocols**, or a ban on brokering lending of unauthorised stablecoins. The ECB wants the yield ban extended to lending and staking — [EBA response to EC MiCA review consultation (2026-09)](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-09/12cb4f01-0d51-46bf-a561-165ac486698f/EBA%20response%20to%20EC%20targeted%20consultation%20on%20MiCA%20review.pdf); [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/mica-under-the-microscope-what-the-eba-and-the-ecb-told-brussels-about-the-revie-102o3sd); [Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/ecb-and-eu-central-banks-want-the-stablecoin-yield-ban-to-reach-lending-and-staking/)

### Inferences
- USDC is issued under Circle's e-money license in the EU, so it is an EMT and Art. 50 applies there. A CASP-like front end offering "interest on USDC" to EU users is high-risk. [background, unverified this session: USDC's MiCA EMT status via Circle Mint France.]
- [background, unverified this session] Celsius (bankrupt July 2022) and Voyager (bankrupt July 2022) show pooled custodial yield failing: commingling, rehypothecation, frozen withdrawals, and retail customers treated as unsecured creditors (the Celsius court held Earn assets were estate property). Kraken settled with the SEC in Nov 2022/Feb 2023 ($30m) over staking-as-a-service. These form the "what not to build" pattern.
- [background, unverified this session] Circle's USDC terms say Circle does not pay holders interest. Third-party yield is the platform's own product and risk.
- Geo-blocking the US, EU/EEA and UK from the yield feature (not the whole social app), using IP checks plus self-attestation, is low-cost and standard. Phrase payouts as variable protocol yield, not "interest".

- The CLARITY Act's Sept 2026 vote result and final rewards language. The OCC GENIUS final rule status (rulemaking was reported for July 2026).
- UK FCA treatment of crypto lending and staking for retail (not researched here).

## Q4. Non-custodial routing vs pooled app vault: does it reduce exposure? Precedents

### Takeaway
Non-custodial design (the user's own zkLogin address deposits directly into a lending protocol and holds the receipt token) removes the BlockFi/Celsius failure mode: no commingling, no platform balance sheet, and assets aren't estate property if ReeF fails. Major wallets ship stablecoin "earn" this way in 2025–2026. It **does not** remove regulatory exposure in Nigeria, though, because the SEC draft explicitly names non-custodial wallet services and yield products. No regulator guidance found here blesses "wallet-routed DeFi yield" as outside licensing.

### Cited Findings
- MetaMask "Money Account" (launched end of June 2026) is marketed as self-custodial, with stablecoin yield from third-party smart-contract vaults lending into DeFi. Trust Wallet's Stablecoin Earn is described as non-custodial, routing to Morpho via Kiln with no lockups. Bitget Wallet offers USDC yield on Aave on Base with on-demand withdrawal. Phantom is non-custodial, but Aave and Morpho savings go through its dApp browser. Coinbase Wallet is non-custodial and separate from exchange USDC rewards, which are custodial. Robinhood's earn feature lends stablecoins from an in-app self-custody wallet into a third-party vault — [search summary incl. Cointelegraph/Trust Wallet interview](https://cointelegraph.com/news/putting-idle-stablecoins-to-work-interview-with-trust-wallet); [Barchart/Bitget](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/35957680/bitget-wallet-stablecoin-earn-tvl-tops-80-million-as-investors-seek-safer-onchain-yields); [Privy docs: earn rewards](https://docs.privy.io/wallets/accounts/capabilities/earn-rewards); [Degate playbook](https://degate.com/playbook/earn-yield-self-custody-wallet/) (mostly marketing or secondary sources)
- Nigerian SEC draft rules cover "non-custodial wallet services" and "yield products" — [The Condia](https://thecondia.com/sec-rules-could-shrink-nigeria-crypto-market/)
- The EBA proposes MiCA requirements for CASPs that "connect clients to DeFi lending protocols", so EU regulators view the front end as the regulatory hook — [EBA response](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-09/12cb4f01-0d51-46bf-a561-165ac486698f/EBA%20response%20to%20EC%20targeted%20consultation%20on%20MiCA%20review.pdf)
- The BlockFi order turned on the platform pooling and lending customer assets itself, which made it an investment company — [SEC 2022-26](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2022-26)

### Inferences
- Rough risk ranking, from most to least defensible: (1) the user signs each deposit and withdrawal from their own address into an established protocol, holds the receipt token (e.g., Suilend cToken, Navi/Scallop sCoin), ReeF takes no spread and has no admin keys over funds. (2) The same, plus an app fee skimmed on-chain, which makes ReeF look like a remunerated intermediary. (3) An app-deployed ERC-4626-style vault where ReeF picks strategies, which looks like portfolio management or a CIS. (4) A custodial pooled vault promising a set rate, which looks like a CIS or deposit-taking and is the BlockFi/Celsius pattern.
- **Fixed-term "locks" create a structural problem.** Sui lending markets offer variable rates. A fixed APY for a term means someone (ReeF, or a fixed-rate protocol) is guaranteeing a return, which moves toward a debt security or deposit. Alternatives: no fixed APY, only a user-chosen self-imposed timelock ("goal lock") with variable yield, or route to an on-chain fixed-rate primitive and disclose that it is that protocol's product.
- ReeF uses Enoki zkLogin plus **sponsored transactions**. Sponsorship means ReeF's backend sees and approves every PTB. That is technically consistent with non-custody, since the user signs and ReeF never holds keys, but it makes ReeF an active facilitator. Document that ReeF cannot move user funds.
- Even the most non-custodial model probably still needs ARIP/SEC engagement in Nigeria, or a partnership with a registered VASP. Non-custody mainly reduces **user-harm and insolvency** risk and US/EU securities characterisation risk, not Nigerian licensing.

### Gaps
- No regulator ruling or no-action letter found on wallet-integrated DeFi yield (US, Nigeria or EU).
- No info on Argent, Zerion or Revolut/Kraken earn structures specifically (not researched in the call budget).

## Q5. User-harm risks and mitigations

### Takeaway
The main risks are smart-contract exploits (Sui's own Cetus hack in May 2025 drained about $223m and hit lending protocols), depeg of USDC, bad debt or insolvency in the lending market, withdrawal freezes at 100% utilisation, and mis-selling "savings" to mainstream users in a market where CBEX-style "guaranteed returns" schemes are common. Mitigations are mostly product-design and disclosure choices.

### Cited Findings
- Cetus (Sui's largest DEX) was exploited on 22 May 2025 for about $223m by injecting near-zero-value tokens through a math/logic bug. Validators froze about $162m (a controversial chain-level intervention), and about $60m was bridged out. The Sui Foundation gave Cetus a loan to compensate users. Sui TVL fell from about $2.13bn to $1.77bn, and Scallop (a Sui lending protocol) temporarily halted services — [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/hacks/cetus-protocol-hit-223-million-hack-162-million-frozen-5-million-bounty-vote-on-c13985eb); [Cointelegraph explainer](https://cointelegraph.com/explained/how-220m-was-stolen-in-minutes-understanding-the-cetus-dex-exploit-on-sui); [Blockworks](https://blockworks.co/news/suis-decentralization-dex-put-to-the-test); [NiftyNoon](https://niftynoon.substack.com/p/niftynoon-the-great-220m-cetus-hack)
- BlockFi was sanctioned specifically for misleading statements about the risk level of its loan portfolio, so risk-disclosure accuracy is itself an enforcement hook — [SEC 2022-26](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2022-26)
- Nigerian draft rules require promotions to be "accurate, fair and not misleading" and a complaints framework — [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/nigerias-proposed-rules-on-digital-and-virtual-assets-key-provisions-and-implication-for-businesses). The ISA Investor Protection Fund does not cover market or performance losses — [Aluko & Oyebode](https://aluko-oyebode.com/insights/do-stable-coins-qualify-as-securities-under-nigerian-law/)
- CBEX red flags: guaranteed returns, aggressive referral rewards, withdrawal problems first — [Dubawa](https://dubawa.org/four-red-flags-nigerians-ignored-until-cbex-crashed/)

### Inferences (design mitigations)
- **Language:** never use "interest", "savings account", "guaranteed" or "risk-free". Use "variable yield from [Protocol], currently X%, changes every block, can go to 0%". Show a 30-day or 7-day trailing realised APY next to the live rate.
- **Utilisation and liquidity:** label "withdraw anytime" as "withdraw when the market has liquidity". Show live pool utilisation. Warn or block deposits when utilisation is above about 90%. Explain that at 100% utilisation withdrawals queue until borrowers repay or are liquidated.
- **Protocol choice:** whitelist audited, long-running Sui lending markets. Cap ReeF-routed TVL per protocol. Run an automatic circuit breaker (pause new deposits) on oracle anomalies, TVL drops or exploit alerts. Consider spreading across protocols.
- **Depeg:** disclose that USDC can lose its peg (it briefly did in March 2023 during SVB [background, unverified this session]). The product doesn't hedge this.
- **Bad debt and exploits:** disclose that losses are socialised by the protocol and ReeF does not reimburse, unless ReeF chooses to fund a disclosed, discretionary buffer. Avoid any "insured" or "protected" wording unless real cover exists. On-chain cover markets (e.g., Nexus Mutual) exist on EVM, but Sui-native cover availability is unverified and limited.
- **Reserve or buffer fund:** a ReeF-funded discretionary buffer improves user outcomes. A *promised* make-whole would turn ReeF into a guarantor (closer to deposit-taking or a debt security). If one exists, keep it explicitly discretionary.
- **Suitability:** add a knowledge quiz or acknowledgement gate before first deposit, low default caps for new or KYC-light users, no referral bonuses tied to deposits (CBEX pattern), and no leverage or loops.
- **Fixed-term:** avoid promising a fixed APY. If a "lock" exists, make it a user-chosen timelock with variable yield and a clear early-exit policy.
- **Ops:** keep a public status page and incident playbook, and show per-user yield statements to support NRS tax reporting.

- Availability of Sui-native smart-contract cover or insurance in 2026.
