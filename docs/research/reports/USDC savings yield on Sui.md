# Wrap lender receipts in user-owned savings locks

ReeF can offer USDC savings in two pots: "Flex" (withdraw any time) and "Lock" (fixed term). The design that holds up is to route each user's USDC from their own zkLogin address into an established Sui lender, then keep the lender's interest-bearing receipt either directly in the user's wallet (Flex) or inside a non-transferable `SavingsLock` object that the user owns (Lock). ReeF never pools funds, never quotes a fixed rate, and never holds a key that can move deposits. The yield is real but modest. Native USDC lending on Sui pays about **4–7% today, and roughly 4–6% of that is sustainable base interest** from borrowers. Anything higher is temporary incentives or curator strategy risk. Withdrawal liquidity is thin: some USDC markets run at 82–94% utilisation, and only a few million dollars sit free in most pools. A locked pot cannot honestly earn more than a flexible one unless ReeF pays a disclosed bonus, so the lock should be sold as a discipline tool, the way PiggyVest's SafeLock is. The bigger constraint is legal, not technical. Nigeria's SEC published draft digital-asset rules on 20 Aug 2026 that explicitly cover yield products *and* non-custodial wallet services aimed at Nigerian residents. Building non-custodially therefore reduces harm to users and US/EU securities exposure, but it does not remove the need for SEC engagement (ARIP) or a registered partner before mainnet. Build the whole feature on testnet now against a mock-yield vault that has the same receipt shape as a real lender, and gate mainnet on an audit, counsel, and geo-blocking of the US, EU and UK.

## Sui lenders pay a 4–6% base, and everything above it is subsidy or strategy risk

Yield on Sui USDC comes overwhelmingly from borrowers paying interest in lending markets. As of 2 Nov 2026, NAVI's native USDC pool paid **6.37% base plus 0.33% rewards**, and Scallop paid **4.02%** with no rewards. Current's MainMarket advertised 6.87%, but only **2.27% of that was base** and the rest was points and token rewards. Current's rewards have made up about two-thirds of its headline since it launched in spring 2026 ([DefiLlama Yields](https://yields.llama.fi/pools); [Current chart](https://yields.llama.fi/chart/a0860ad8-ba2a-4ff8-8a13-348788864f23)). Suilend is the largest lender at **$143.2M TVL**, but DefiLlama tracks none of its pools. The best available figure comes from a competitor's blog dated 12 Oct 2026, which reports about 4.38% base supply APR on its Main Market USDC at **82.3% utilisation** and about 5.71% on its Ember Market at **94.0% utilisation**. That figure is unverified ([JewelSwap](https://www.jewelswap.io/blog/how-to-use-suilend); [DefiLlama protocols](https://api.llama.fi/protocols)).

Sources for the table: [DefiLlama Yields](https://yields.llama.fi/pools), [NAVI history](https://yields.llama.fi/chart/0fddbf5d-ec14-4570-80d3-a70c85573d3e), [Scallop history](https://yields.llama.fi/chart/ddf68725-6ced-4c27-90ca-c14752cd7218), [DefiLlama hacks](https://api.llama.fi/hacks), [Sui DeepBook Margin docs](https://docs.sui.io/onchain-finance/deepbook-margin/contract-information.md), [Ember chart](https://yields.llama.fi/chart/a1a9916c-63b2-49a1-b4b0-cbe8a9e7275c).

Rates have come down a long way. NAVI's USDC supply rate averaged **29.9% in March 2024**, when it was almost all incentives. It has sat between **4.7% and 5.9% every month of 2026**. Daily rates still swing widely, for example 2.9–22.2% within April 2026, and spikes reached 88% in November 2025 ([DefiLlama NAVI chart](https://yields.llama.fi/chart/0fddbf5d-ec14-4570-80d3-a70c85573d3e)). Two things follow for product design. ReeF should present a trailing 7- or 30-day average, never the spot rate. And it should plan around a **4–5% base**, with dips toward 2.5% and occasional double-digit spikes when demand for leverage surges.

The sector is also shrinking. Sui lending TVL fell **20.4%, to $355.6M**, between 1 May and 30 Jun 2026. Only **$94.7M of USDC was supplied** across all protocols, against **$73.4M borrowed**, which is about 77% utilisation for USDC as a whole ([Datumlab](https://www.datumlab.xyz/resources/reports/state-of-lending-sui-may-june-2026)). For a consumer app, the binding constraint is therefore exit liquidity, not yield. Suilend caps outflows at about $5M an hour on its Main Market and $2M on Ember, and when a market reaches 100% utilisation, withdrawals fail until borrowers repay ([JewelSwap](https://www.jewelswap.io/blog/how-to-use-suilend)).

Several sources that look attractive should not be ReeF's default. Ember's curated vaults advertise 8–24%, but the returns come from basis trades, RWA credit and concentrated liquidity. DefiLlama flagged on 19 Oct 2026 that Ember's R25 Treasury vault was marked about **5% above its on-chain backing** ([DefiLlama RWA mrcUSD](https://defillama.com/rwa/asset/mrcUSD); [Bluefin docs](https://learn.bluefin.io/bluefin/structured-vaults/introduction)). USDY is the only tokenised T-bill natively on Sui, but primary access requires KYC and a $100K minimum, and the secondary pool on Cetus holds only about $2M ([Eco](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15254006-ousg-vs-buidl-vs-usdy-yield-2026-tokenized-t-bills-compared); [DefiLlama Yields](https://yields.llama.fi/pools)). USDsui, the Bridge/Stripe-issued Sui dollar that launched on 4 Mar 2026, sends its reserve yield to SUI buybacks and ecosystem incentives rather than to holders ([Tangem](https://tangem.com/en/news/stablecoins/16859-sui-launches-usdsui-stablecoin-redirects-yield-to-ecosystem/)). There is no fixed-rate USDC market on Sui. Nemo was the Pendle-style candidate, and it was drained of **$2.59M in September 2025** and now has zero TVL ([DefiLlama hacks](https://api.llama.fi/hacks)). That gap matters for the Lock pot: no fixed-rate venue exists on Sui to back a fixed-rate lock.

Two data gaps should be closed before choosing a mainnet lender. DefiLlama names Current's pools "MainMarket" and "EmberMarket", the same names the JewelSwap guide gives Suilend's markets, and the research could not tell whether the two protocols are related or one source mislabels the other. AlphaLend (Bluefin) dropped out of DefiLlama coverage in June 2026, even though Datumlab still tracked $55.2M in it ([Datumlab](https://www.datumlab.xyz/resources/reports/state-of-lending-sui-may-june-2026)).

## Exploits and admin keys, not rates, decide which lender is safe enough

Sui's defining incident is the **Cetus exploit of 22 May 2025, about $223M**. Validators froze about $162M, a governance vote with about 90% approval moved the frozen funds to a multisig, and the Sui Foundation lent Cetus **$30M USDC** so it could relaunch about 17 days later ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/356347/sui-community-passes-governance-vote-to-recover-stolen-cetus-funds-blocked-by-validators-in-223m-heist); [The Block relaunch](https://www.theblock.co/post/357386/sui-dex-cetus-protocol-restarts-platform-after-recovering-from-223-million-exploit)). Scallop temporarily halted services during the event ([Blockworks](https://blockworks.co/news/suis-decentralization-dex-put-to-the-test)). The recovery shows the chain has an emergency backstop, but that backstop depended on validator coordination and a vote, and it would not help with slow bad debt.

The 2026 incidents are smaller but more relevant to a savings product. Volo Vault lost **$3.5M on 21 Apr 2026** through a compromised admin private key, even though it had been audited three times ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/volo-defi-3-5m-exploit-vault-attack-recovery)). Scallop lost $142K to a reward-logic flaw on 26 Apr 2026. Typus Perp lost $3.44M to an oracle failure in October 2025. DefiLlama lists **no incidents for NAVI, Suilend or Current** ([DefiLlama hacks](https://api.llama.fi/hacks)). Datumlab recorded no bad debt or pauses in May–June 2026. It did record a NAVI liquidation that priced wUSDT at **$60,481 per unit**, after which NAVI added a $0.50–$2.00 price guard for stablecoins ([Datumlab](https://www.datumlab.xyz/resources/reports/state-of-lending-sui-may-june-2026)). The research found **no credible insurance for Sui USDC deposits**.

The lesson from Volo bears directly on ReeF's architecture. In 2026, the live threat on Sui was a single privileged key sitting over pooled funds, and that is exactly what ReeF would create if it built a shared vault. The admin-key, upgrade-cap and timelock setup of each candidate lender was not verified and should be a gating checklist item. Suilend's audit history (Zellic and OtterSec in 2024, Certora formal verification in Feb 2026) is the strongest documented, but its 2025 losses in "isolated pools" are mentioned without detail ([JewelSwap](https://www.jewelswap.io/blog/how-to-use-suilend); [Suilend audits](https://docs.suilend.fi/security/audit)).

Testnet support is weak. The research could not confirm that any major lender runs a stable native-USDC market on Sui testnet. A June 2026 press release announced a "Hashi" global testnet with AlphaLend, Bluefin, Current, Scallop and Suilend for July 2026, but whether it is live has not been checked ([Chainwire via MetaTrader](https://www.metatrader.com/zh/news/chainwire/1778844-sui-news-cumberland-fluid-and-swissborg-join)). DeepBook Margin publishes testnet contract info ([Sui docs](https://docs.sui.io/onchain-finance/deepbook-margin/contract-information.md)). The SDKs are current: `@suilend/sdk` 14.2.0, `@naviprotocol/lending` 2.1.0, `@scallop-io/sui-scallop-sdk` 5.4.3 and `@mysten/deepbook-v3` 2.9.1 were all published within the last month. However, npm shows `@mysten/sui` at **2.38.0** while ReeF's CLAUDE.md locks 2.19.0, so peer-version conflicts are likely when ReeF integrates them.

## Owned lock objects beat both direct calls and a pooled vault

Three architectures are possible, and they trade custody against control.

| | (a) Direct protocol calls | (b) ReeF pooled vault | (c) User-owned `SavingsLock` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who holds funds | User holds the receipt token in their zkLogin address | Shared ReeF object holds everyone's USDC | User owns an object that wraps their own receipt |
| Can enforce a lock | No | Yes | Yes |
| Can pay a bonus | Only from an off-chain ledger | Yes | Yes, via a separate bonus pool |
| Enoki allowlist | Third-party targets that change when lenders upgrade | ReeF targets only, stable | ReeF targets (plus the lender's, if called directly in the PTB) |
| Legal posture | Most defensible | Looks like a collective investment scheme (CIS) or deposit-taking | Close to (a), but ReeF code decides when funds unlock |
| Fit with D-1 (owned-first) | Yes | Contradicts it | Yes |

Option (b) is the BlockFi pattern. The SEC fined BlockFi **$100M** in 2022 because the platform pooled customer assets and lent them out itself, which made BlockFi an unregistered investment company ([SEC 2022-26](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2022-26)). In Nigeria, a pooled ReeF vault maps onto the broad CIS definition in ISA 2025 s.150 ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/venture-capital/1638148/nigerias-investments-and-securities-act-2025-what-is-changing-for-private-capital-players)). It would also put a Volo-style admin key over everyone's money. Option (a) is how the major wallets ship stablecoin earn in 2026. Trust Wallet routes to Morpho through Kiln with no lockups, Bitget Wallet routes to Aave, and MetaMask's Money Account routes to third-party vaults ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/putting-idle-stablecoins-to-work-interview-with-trust-wallet); [Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/35957680/bitget-wallet-stablecoin-earn-tvl-tops-80-million-as-investors-seek-safer-onchain-yields)). But option (a) cannot enforce a lock.

The right design is therefore **(a) for Flex and (c) for Lock**, with both sitting behind ReeF wrapper entry points. The `SavingsLock` struct has the `key` ability but not `store`. That makes it non-transferable: only the ReeF module can unwrap it, and the module checks `unlock_ms` against the shared `Clock` at `0x6`. One Move detail matters here. A PTB's `TransferObjects` command requires `store`, so the module must do the transfer itself, through an `open_lock` function that sends the object to `ctx.sender()`. Under the deposit-into-lender design, ReeF's own state is uncontended. The deposit still touches the lender's shared market object, though, so the parallelism benefit is that ReeF adds no contention of its own, not that the transaction skips consensus. To stay true to D-1, ReeF should keep its penalty parameters as constants or in a frozen (immutable) config object rather than a mutable shared one.

The lender's receipt token removes the need to write share accounting. Scallop's sCoin and Suilend's cToken work like Compound's cToken. Supply changes only when someone deposits or redeems, and the exchange rate (total underlying divided by supply) rises as borrowers pay interest ([deepwiki Scallop](https://deepwiki.com/scallop-io/sui-lending-protocol/4.1-supplying-and-redeeming-assets-(minting-scoin)); [Zellic Suilend](https://reports.zellic.io/publications/suilend/sections/3-reserve-function-deposit-liquidity-and-mint-ctokens-deposit-liquidity-and-mint-ctokens)). ReeF only stores `principal` for display and penalties. If ReeF ever builds a pooled vault, it would have to implement ERC-4626-style math with `u128` intermediates and protect against the first-depositor inflation attack with virtual shares or dead shares. Sources disagree on whether OpenZeppelin's default offset of 0 is enough. OpenZeppelin calls the attack "non-profitable", while a Hacken report shows victims still losing 9–25% ([OpenZeppelin](https://www.openzeppelin.com/news/a-novel-defense-against-erc4626-inflation-attacks); [Hacken](https://hackenproof.com/reports/OPENEDCA-112)). That is one more reason to avoid option (b).

There is an awkward trade-off in choosing which lender to wrap. Scallop's sCoin is the easiest receipt to hold inside an object, because it is a plain fungible `Coin`. But Scallop is also the weakest lender on the list: about $0.6M of free liquidity, TVL down to $9.0M, and an exploit in April 2026. NAVI pays the best base rate, but its positions live in protocol storage against an address or an AccountCap rather than as a coin. Wrapping a NAVI position would mean storing an AccountCap in the lock, which has not been verified. Suilend is the largest lender and has no listed incidents, but the research did not confirm whether its cTokens can be held outside an Obligation. The first engineering task for mainnet is therefore a one-day check of each lender's source code. Until that is done, every lender should sit behind the same adapter interface.

### Penalties should forfeit yield, never principal

Nigerian incumbents have already set the rules users expect. Breaking a PiggyVest Target before maturity costs the accrued interest plus a **1% fee**, and a "locked target" cannot be broken at all ([PiggyVest blog](https://blog.piggyvest.com/save/how-piggyvest-interest-rates-work/)). Binance locked products forfeit all rewards on early redemption ([Binance FAQ](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/af607a50de614350ae57be3e6f616699)). Cowrywise locks plans to maturity but keeps an Emergency plan that can be withdrawn any time ([Cowrywise](https://help.cowrywise.com/en/articles/1624208-types-of-savings-plans-on-cowrywise)). In Move, `break_early` should return receipt tokens worth `principal` at the current exchange rate and divert the rest. One edge case must be handled: if the lender takes bad debt and the rate falls, then `principal / rate` will exceed the receipt the lock holds, and the function must return everything rather than abort. The lock object should also carry a `breakable: bool` flag. Any emergency unlock on an unbreakable lock must be disclosed, or there should be no emergency unlock at all.

Where forfeited yield goes matters legally. If it goes to a ReeF treasury, ReeF becomes a paid intermediary, which moves it down the regulatory ranking (see the regulatory section). A better choice is to send forfeits to a `BonusPool` that pays people who hold to maturity. That needs a shared object, but only on the cold break and claim paths.

### A locked pot earns more only if someone pays for it

With no fixed-rate market on Sui, a Lock pot earns exactly what Flex earns unless one of three things funds a bonus. The first is lender incentive tokens, such as NAVI claims, Scallop sSCA or Suilend sSUI/SEND, which ReeF could harvest and pay only to lockers. These are real but volatile. The second is a ReeF bonus funded from revenue, such as the DeepBook take-rate or tip fees, shown as a separate line from the lender's rate. The third is redistributing early-break forfeits. Every one of these turns ReeF into a payer of a return. MiCA's definition of "interest" covers any benefit tied to how long a holder keeps the token ([ESMA Art. 50](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-50-prohibition-granting-interest)). The recommendation is to **launch Lock with no bonus**, framed as "save with discipline", the way PiggyVest built its SafeLock brand, and add a discretionary, capped ReeF bonus only after counsel signs off. A **0% Halal lock** that never touches a lender, mirroring Cowrywise's Halal plan, costs almost nothing to build and serves a large Nigerian segment ([Cowrywise Halal](https://cowrywise.com/blog/halal-accounts/)).

### Sponsorship pitfalls are specific and fixable

Savings can use the same sponsorship path as the rest of ReeF. A `savingsAllowedTargets()` set gets appended to the allowlist in `services/indexer/src/sponsor.ts`, and it should cover only ReeF's `savings::{deposit_flex, withdraw_flex, open_lock, withdraw_matured, break_early}` plus whatever lender and oracle targets the chosen adapter needs. NAVI withdrawals may require price-update calls in the same PTB, which has not been verified. Three bugs are predictable. First, the deposit PTB must split from the user's USDC coin and not from `tx.gas`, because the gas coin belongs to Enoki. Second, users who receive many tips end up with fragmented USDC coin objects, so the PTB should `mergeCoins` first. Third, wrappers must restrict the coin type to `USDC` in Move, and the sponsor endpoint should rate-limit per sender to stop spam. The fetched Enoki docs do not explain whether the allowlist also gates commands like `SplitCoins`, so ReeF should confirm this against its existing DeepBook Predict sponsorship, which already works ([Enoki docs](https://docs.enoki.mystenlabs.com/ts-sdk/sponsored-transactions)).

## Lagos savers expect named pots, maturity dates and honest break rules

PiggyVest set the template for Nigerian savers. Its products are named pots: Flex (withdraw any time), Target (goal-based, about 12%), SafeLock (up to **18.5%**, scaled by duration) and Flex Dollar (about **7%**). Rates are pegged to the CBN Monetary Policy Rate ([TechCabal 2025](https://techcabal.com/2025/08/22/fintech-apps-in-nigeria-with-competitive-interest-rates-on-savings/)). SafeLock's best-known engagement hook was paying interest upfront at the moment of locking ([TechCabal 2020](https://techcabal.com/2020/09/10/the-backend-piggyvest-engagement-safelock-security/)). Risevest's dollar product offers 3-, 6- and 12-month terms at about 10% historically, "not guaranteed" and paid at maturity, backed by sovereign Eurobonds ([Risevest](https://risevest.com/fixed-income)). Crypto apps add the language of variability. Coinbase pays USDC "rewards" that are accrued daily, paid weekly and "subject to change", and it states that balances are not deposit accounts ([Coinbase help](https://help.coinbase.com/en/coinbase/derivatives/boosted-usdc-rewards)).

ReeF's framing problem is that **4–6% looks low next to 12–18% naira rates**. The fair comparison is with dollar products: PiggyVest Flex Dollar at about 7% and Risevest at about 10%. On that comparison, the main thing ReeF offers is protection against naira devaluation, with yield as the secondary benefit. The savings home screen should show USDC with a ≈₦ equivalent and a balance ticker that interpolates each second from the trailing APY. Interpolation is needed because lenders only accrue interest when someone interacts with the market. Each Lock option (1, 3, 6 or 12 months) should show the lender's variable rate, with any ReeF bonus on a separate line. The break-early screen should state the exact number computed from the on-chain formula: "you get back $X, you give up $Y earned". The social hook is "save 10% of every tip". Tips are ReeF's strongest on-ramp, because no confirmed provider sells NGN for USDC on the Sui network. Banxa, Coinbase, OnRamp.money and Meld support USDC on Sui, but none publishes country coverage ([Sui docs on-ramps](https://docs.sui.io/onchain-finance/asset-custody/fiat-on-ramps)). Recurring auto-save cannot pull funds from a self-custodial wallet without a signature, so it has to work as a reminder plus a one-tap sponsored transaction. Whether a delegated session key could do this under zkLogin has not been verified.

The disclosure rules come straight from Nigeria's history of collapsed schemes. CBEX promised roughly 100% returns, grew through referral rewards, and collapsed in April 2025 with alleged losses of ₦1.3tn. Another estimate puts the losses at about $12M ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/04/15/cbex-ponzi-collapse-nigeria/); [Dubawa](https://dubawa.org/four-red-flags-nigerians-ignored-until-cbex-crashed/)). ReeF must therefore never use the words "interest", "guaranteed", "insured" or "risk-free". It must never pay referral bonuses tied to deposits. It should show live utilisation, warn or block deposits above about 90% utilisation, describe Flex as "withdraw when the market has liquidity", and require an acknowledgement quiz and low default caps before a user's first deposit. BlockFi was penalised partly for misstating its loan-portfolio risk ([SEC 2022-26](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2022-26)), so accurate risk copy is itself a legal requirement, not only good UX.

## Non-custody cuts user harm but not Nigerian licensing exposure

The Investments and Securities Act 2025, in force since 25 Mar 2025, defines virtual and digital assets as securities and gives the SEC power over VASPs ([PwC Nigeria](https://www.pwc.com/ng/en/publications/summary-of-key-changes-investments-securities-act-2025.html); [Aluko & Oyebode](https://aluko-oyebode.com/insights/do-stable-coins-qualify-as-securities-under-nigerian-law/)). The SEC's **proposed** Rules on Digital and Virtual Asset Operations, Custody and Markets were published on 20 Aug 2026 and had not been adopted in any source found. Commentary on the draft says it covers **staking, lending, yield products and non-custodial wallet services** ([SEC Nigeria](https://sec.gov.ng/for-investors/proposed-rules-digital-and-virtual-assets-operations-custody-and-markets/); [The Condia](https://thecondia.com/sec-rules-could-shrink-nigeria-crypto-market/)). The draft reaches anyone serving Nigerian residents "through digital channels". It requires Nigerian incorporation with a resident CEO and principal officers, and it sends new entrants through ARIP, which grants an Approval-in-Principle for up to two years ([Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/nigerias-proposed-rules-on-digital-and-virtual-assets-key-provisions-and-implication-for-businesses)). It also drops the lighter "ancillary VASP" category for pure software providers, which removes the obvious "we are just an interface" defence. Capital requirements conflict between sources. SEC Circular 26-1 (January 2026) sets ₦2bn for exchanges and custodians and ₦300M for an ancillary VASP, with a deadline of 30 Jun 2027 ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2026/01/16/sec-raises-capital-requirement-for-crypto-exchanges-in-nigeria-to-n2-billion/)). The draft proposes a ₦30M registration fee and ₦200M for a general VASP ([Leadership.ng](https://leadership.ng/sec-proposes-n30m-registration-fee-n2bn-capital-requirement-for-crypto-exchanges/)). The research did not extract the draft's specific conditions on yield products, and the full SEC PDF needs to be read.

Enforcement in Nigeria falls on individuals. Binance executive Tigran Gambaryan was detained for about eight months in 2024 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/322614/nigeria-drops-money-laundering-charges-against-binance-executive-tigran-gambaryan-report)). After CBEX, the SEC Director-General said the platform "is not registered with us" and moved to freeze accounts with the police and EFCC ([TheCable](https://www.thecable.ng/just-in-cbex-not-registered-with-us-says-sec-dg)). Press reports cite up to 10 years in prison for unregistered investment platforms, though that figure was not checked against the Act ([Leadership.ng](https://leadership.ng/unregistered-digital-investment-platforms-face-10-yr-jail-term-sec-cracks-down-amid-outrage-over-cbex-crash)). Tax has also changed. From 1 Jan 2026, crypto gains are taxed as income, and entities that exchange or custody crypto must register as VASPs and report customer data. Reported penalties are ₦10M in the first month of default, and sources disagree on individual rates ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/09/18/nigeria-crypto-tax-law-vasp-penalties-2026/)). ReeF's indexer should therefore produce per-user yield statements from day one. Whether paying yield on a "withdraw any time" or "fixed term" product counts as deposit-taking under BOFIA 2020 was not answered by any source, so the CBN dimension stays open.

Outside Nigeria the picture is consistent. The GENIUS Act (P.L. 119-27) bars stablecoin *issuers* from paying yield. Whether platforms may pay "rewards" was still being fought over in the CLARITY Act as of September 2026, and the outcome of the 15 Sep vote was not verified ([CRS](https://www.legistorm.com/reports/view/crs/608234/The_Stablecoin_Yield_Debate.html); [Invezz](https://invezz.com/au/news/2026/09/14/clarity-act-senate-vote-on-tuesday-what-to-expect/)). MiCA Article 50 bars both issuers and CASPs from paying interest on e-money tokens. The EBA's September 2026 review response floats new rules for CASPs that "connect clients to DeFi lending protocols" ([EBA](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-09/12cb4f01-0d51-46bf-a561-165ac486698f/EBA%20response%20to%20EC%20targeted%20consultation%20on%20MiCA%20review.pdf)). In other words, regulators now treat the front-end app as the point of control. Geo-blocking the US, EU/EEA and UK from the savings feature, while leaving the social app open, is cheap and standard. UK treatment was not researched.

From most to least defensible, the designs rank as follows. First, the user signs each deposit into an established lender and ReeF takes no spread and holds no keys. Second, the same with an on-chain ReeF fee. Third, a ReeF-run strategy vault. Fourth, a custodial pool that promises a rate. The recommended Flex-plus-Lock design sits in the first tier, provided early-break forfeits do not flow to ReeF. Sponsorship makes ReeF an active facilitator that approves every transaction, so its terms should state plainly that ReeF cannot move user funds. Even this design most likely needs ARIP registration or a registered Nigerian VASP partner before mainnet. Building non-custodially protects users and reduces US/EU exposure, but it does not remove the need for a Nigerian licence.

## Build it on testnet now, behind an adapter, and gate mainnet on law and audit

The build sequence follows from the fact that ReeF is testnet-only and no lender has been confirmed on testnet. Engineering can go far ahead of the legal work, because the mock and the real lender share one interface.

| Step | Work | Exit criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Move: `reef::test_usdc` (testnet only) and `reef::mock_yield`, a shared `MockVault` with an index that accrues on each interaction, minting a `MOCK_SUSDC` receipt shaped like Scallop's sCoin. Top it up with the existing `faucet.ts` pattern. | Mock receipt rate rises with time in unit tests |
| 2 | Move: `reef::savings` with `deposit_flex`/`withdraw_flex`, plus `open_lock`, `withdraw_matured` and `break_early` on a key-only `SavingsLock`. Includes the `breakable` flag, forfeit-yield penalty, falling-rate edge case, USDC-only type check, frozen config, and events. | Tests cover maturity, early break, rate drop, and wrong coin type |
| 3 | `@umbra/core/savings` adapter interface (`buildDeposit`, `buildRedeem`, `exchangeRate`, `apy`, `allowedTargets`) with a mock adapter. Wire `allowedTargets()` into `sponsor.ts`. Merge coins and split from user USDC. | Gasless deposit, lock, break and withdraw on testnet |
| 4 | Indexer: savings events, cost basis per user, trailing 7d/30d APY, utilisation feed, per-user yield statements. | "Earned" figure matches on-chain value within rounding |
| 5 | Web and mobile: Flex and Lock pots, ₦ display, ticker, break-early preview, risk-acknowledgement gate, geo-gate, 0% Halal lock, "save 10% of tips". | Usability test with Lagos users |
| 6 | Mainnet spike: verify Suilend cToken representation, NAVI AccountCap wrapping, Scallop sCoin, current package IDs, oracle calls, admin and upgrade keys, and `@mysten/sui` peer versions. Pick a primary lender and one backup. | Written adapter decision |
| 7 | Gate: audit of `savings`, Nigerian counsel on the SEC draft, ARIP or a VASP partner, per-protocol caps tied to free liquidity, circuit breaker on oracle, TVL or exploit alerts, public status page. | Limited mainnet beta with low per-user caps |
| 8 | Later: discretionary ReeF bonus pool, spreading deposits across lenders, NGN on-ramp partner after confirming Sui support. | Only after legal sign-off |

Several trade-offs should be decided explicitly. Calling the lender directly in the PTB keeps ReeF's code simple, but it puts third-party targets on the allowlist, and those can break silently when the lender upgrades. NAVI has already moved its entry point from `incentive_v2` to `incentive_v3` ([NAVI docs](https://naviprotocol.gitbook.io/navi-protocol-developer-docs/smart-contract-overview/lending-core)). Calling the lender through a Move dependency keeps the allowlist to ReeF targets only, but ReeF must republish whenever the lender's interface changes. With a single lender, direct PTB calls plus allowlist monitoring are cheaper. Once ReeF spreads deposits across lenders, the wrapper pays for itself. Similarly, a lender with high incentives such as Current shows a better number on screen, but its base rate is only 2.27%, so the product would quietly underdeliver when the incentives end. The default lender should be chosen on base rate and free liquidity, with incentives treated as a bonus.

## Conclusion

USDC savings on Sui is well within reach technically and narrow economically. The real product is a dollar hedge that earns about 4–6%, wrapped in the commitment habits Nigerian savers already trust. Two conclusions are less obvious. First, the receipt token that is easiest to engineer around belongs to the riskiest lender, so the adapter boundary and the source-code check in step 6 matter more than the choice of a launch lender. Second, the features that make a lock attractive (a higher rate, upfront interest, redistributed penalties) are exactly the ones that turn ReeF from a software interface into a party paying a return. ReeF should ship the lock as a commitment device first and add yield to it only with a regulator's approval.

The legal path is clear in direction but not yet in detail. The Nigerian SEC draft has not been adopted, and its yield-product conditions are unread. Either could change the answer between "partner with a registered VASP" and "register directly through ARIP". None of that blocks testnet work. Everything in steps 1–5 can ship now, and step 7 decides whether mainnet launches in Nigeria at all.
